## 4. Specification checks: is the level correlation more than a shared trend?

Both variables rise across the observed window, so a correlation in levels is expected whether or not the two are related. Three specifications are reported and their disagreement is treated as the finding rather than resolved by picking a favourite:

- **levels** — weekly values as published (the primary specification)
- **first differences** — week-over-week changes; removes any shared trend, but is noisy and has low power if the true relationship operates on levels
- **linear detrended** — residuals after removing a straight-line time trend; note this models a flat-then-step price path poorly, so it does not fully remove the confound either

Bold marks significance at α = 0.05.

**No series survives first differencing** (0 of 6 significant at α = 0.05). Week-to-week changes in crude prices and EV search interest show no detectable relationship in any market. Linear detrending disagrees for several series, which is expected given the step-shaped price path, so the two checks bound rather than settle the question.

This is the single most important limitation in the analysis. Every significant level correlation below is classified `level_only_association`, and no series reaches the `robust_positive_association` classification. The honest reading is that crude prices and EV interest both rose over these seven months, and this dataset cannot separate a genuine relationship from two trends that happened to coincide.

## 12. Limitations that must be disclosed

1. **Sample size.** 30 weekly pairs. Every interval is wide and every coefficient is imprecise.
2. **Regime imbalance.** Only 4 weeks sit in the elevated-price regime, against 26 baseline weeks. The oil variable has almost no variation outside that short episode.
3. **No cross-series level comparison is possible.** Each Trends export is rescaled to its own maximum. Statements ranking markets by interest level are unavailable from these files at any level of effort.
4. **Correlation only.** No confounder is measured or controlled: seasonality, model launches, news volume, advertising, policy announcements. No causal claim is supportable.
5. **Crude, not retail.** The price variable is a global crude benchmark. Consumers respond to pump prices, which differ by country through tax and subsidy regimes and are absent from this dataset.
6. **One partial week.** The oil week beginning 2026-03-22 rests on fewer than five trading days and is flagged in every artifact.
7. **Trends provenance is incomplete.** The original project did not record the exact query terms, category filter or retrieval dates for the six exports, so the Google Trends half of this analysis is not fully reproducible from source.
8. **Search interest is not adoption.** The dependent variable measures searching, not buying.
